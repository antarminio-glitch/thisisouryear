# Homepage proposal

Make choosing a city the main event. A phone visitor should understand the shirt, see the price, and reach their colors without passing a photo gallery.

## Critique of the starting page

- **Hero:** The green field and gold OUR YEAR are the right identity. Two equally strong buttons split attention. The model photos make the hero much taller before anyone can choose a city.
- **Find:** The empty state is a good invitation. On phones, its large square takes up space before the search field. The picker already handles the actual purchase well.
- **Ways in:** Tees, hoodies, and kids repeat choices available in the picker. Matching sets, fantasy punishment, and rival gifts deserve more than small list entries. Bandwagon has no permanent invitation.
- **Cities:** Seeing every colorway is useful, but 32 shirts create a long detour and duplicate city search.
- **Fans:** Approved customer photos are useful evidence. Keeping the section hidden until photos exist is honest.
- **How it works:** A useful explanation, but the checkout speed and delivery language make promises the homepage cannot establish.

## What changed

1. **Hero:** Kept the green field, white THIS IS, and gold OUR YEAR. Shortened the copy and spacing. “Find my colors” is the sole prominent button; a smaller link leads gift shoppers to the other buying ideas. The adult tee starting price comes from the existing catalog.
2. **Find:** Immediately follows the hero. Kept the no-shirt empty state and made it compact on phones. Once a city is picked, the full preview returns. Existing garment, fit, size, and cart controls remain.
3. **More ways to say it:** Four whole-card links sell matching sets, fantasy punishment, rival gifts, and bandwagon. Each explains the occasion. Garment categories stay in the picker and shop.
4. **Browse cities:** Kept the searchable 32-city gallery inside an expandable section. Browsers can explore; everyone else can continue down the page.
5. **Wearing it:** Moved the two existing model mockups here, with an explicit mockup note and a return link to the picker.
6. **Fans and how it works:** Kept approved fan photos and the three-step explanation. Replaced unsupported timing claims with size-chart and shipping guidance. Confirm placeholders and the legal footer stay unchanged.

## Options, ranked

1. **Picker first, compact hero — this proposal.** Fastest path to a personal preview and purchase. Product photography arrives later.
2. **One static shirt or model beside the desktop hero.** Shows the product sooner, but an arbitrary city's colors can look like the only option. Adds phone height.
3. **Rotating city-shirt showcase.** Demonstrates variety, but motion and extra browsing compete with picking a city. More controls and maintenance.
4. **Full city gallery before the picker.** Strong visual choice, but too much scrolling for a visitor who knows their city.

## First A/B test

Compare this compact hero with the same hero plus one static product photo. Keep everything else identical. Measure city selections, then add-to-cart and completed orders per homepage visit, especially on phones. Photo clicks alone would not establish a winner.
